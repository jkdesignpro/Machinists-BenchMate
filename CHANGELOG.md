# Changelog

All notable changes to Machinists BenchMate. Versions follow semver.

## [Unreleased]
### Added (the 0.2.0 overhaul: shell and look)
- **Modes**: a Simple / Advanced / Expert selector in the top bar, remembered between runs. Simple lists 16 everyday tools with only their main inputs; Advanced lists every tool; Expert also opens every working, constant and evidence table. A mode never changes a calculation.
- **Workspaces**: the tools are regrouped by the job into Turning, Milling, Holes and threads, Grinding, sawing and gears, Measure and inspect, Shop maths, Programming and Reference, each with a landing page of cards. Related tools share one page with tabs (for example Feed conversions, Hole patterns, Limits and fits, True position, DXF shapes), so 151 tools sit on 123 pages.
- **Home dashboard**: a page of widgets you add, remove, resize and reorder (drag, or the arrow buttons). Widgets: any calculator in small form, favourites, recent tools, pinned results, the calculator, tip of the day, tool library and what's new. The first run asks what you mostly do and builds a starter layout.
- **Calculator panel**: the calculator opens over any page from the top bar or with Alt+C, and from the small calculator button in any number box, which puts the answer back into that box.
- **Calculator: lengths with units.** Type `12.7mm + 1/2 in`, `3 7/16"`, `5' 3 1/2"`, `5 thou` or `25 um` and the answer comes back in the app's unit, with the other unit beside it. Units: in, ft, thou, mm, cm, m, um. All conversions use the exact definitions (1 in = 25.4 mm, 1 ft = 12 in).
- **Calculator: degrees, minutes and seconds** typed as `29°30'15"`, anywhere an angle goes.
- **Calculator: Speed function** in the functions drawer: diameter with surface speed gives RPM, or RPM gives surface speed, in the app's units.
- Calculator: curly quotes, primes and the printed × ÷ − signs are read as typed or pasted. Bolt circle coordinates that should be 0 no longer show as a tiny number such as 6e-17.
- **Common controls on every calculator**: a large main result, more or fewer decimal places, reset, copy the results, pin a result (with its inputs) to Home, favourite star, and "Formula and sources". Boxes that must hold a number are marked when empty.
- **Linked pairs**: on Spindle speed, Milling feed, Lathe feed, C-axis feed and Ball-nose cusp, type in either box of a pair (for example surface speed or RPM) and the other is worked out.
- **Search** in the top bar (Ctrl+K or /) across every tool and tab, with keyboard selection.
- **Compact view**: one column, the tool list as a drawer and a bottom bar, for a narrow window. Auto (below 820 px), On or Off in the view menu.
- **What's new** page built from this changelog, and a **tip of the day** (BenchMate's own tips).
- **Drawing kit** (`app/dk.js`): one engineering-drawing style for all drawings (part outlines, centre lines, dimension lines, angle arcs, leaders, hatching) that follows the light and dark themes; first drawings for Spindle speed and Bolt circle.
- `tests/smoke/shot.mjs`: takes a screenshot of any screen for checking layouts and drawings.
### Changed (the 0.2.0 overhaul: shell and look)
- New look of BenchMate's own: drafting blue and marking-out orange on cool paper, a blueprint dark theme, larger type and controls, cards and a faint drafting grid. Same bones as before: top bar, left rail, content area, token CSS.
- The address now follows the screen (`#/home`, `#/w/turning`, `#/t/rpm`), so Back and Forward work and the app reopens where you left it. Input values are kept between runs.
- Smoke test rewritten for the new shell: first-run question, dashboard changes kept after a restart, every tool in all three modes and both unit systems, Compact view at 420 px, search, theme, linked pairs and the calculator.
### Added (improved tools)
- **Tap drill size**: thread engagement slider (55 to 85 %, value shown) and a Cut tap / Form tap choice. Each tap type keeps its own engagement, starting at 75 % for cutting and 65 % for forming (USCTI and FM Carbide for cutting; Besly and FM Carbide for forming, quoted as ranges). The result shows the theoretical hole for both tap types and, for each, the nearest number, letter, fractional and metric drill below and above with the difference and the engagement that drill really gives.
- **Spot and centre drill depth**: pick a standard centre drill by type and size: inch plain (#000 to #10, 60, 82 or 90°) and bell type (#11 to #20), metric type A (ISO 866 / DIN 333 A), type B (ISO 2540) and type R (ISO 2541). Shows body, pilot diameter, pilot length (a range for metric), bell or d2 diameter and included angle, each with its publishers and status, the largest countersink the size can make, the depth to reach your countersink diameter (for both ends of a pilot length range) and the diameter at a depth. Data in `data/refdata/centredrills-evidence.json` from seven publishers and two ISO previews; single-source and differing values are marked.
- `benchmate-core`: `spot_chamfer_dia` (diameter a pointed tool cuts at a depth; was worked out in the screen) with tests, and table-size tests for `centre_drill_depth`.
- **Chord, arc and sagitta**: enter any two of radius, included angle, chord, sagitta and arc length (all ten pairs) and get the rest with segment and sector areas. Chord + arc length and sagitta + arc length are solved in the Rust core by bisection on a bracket where the function is monotonic; where two arcs fit (radius + chord, and some sagitta + arc length pairs) both are offered. The 3-point circle is unchanged.
- `benchmate-core`: `arc_from_two`, `arc_two_flat`, `arc_sl_peak` and a general `bisect`, with tests that rebuild minor and major arcs from every pair.
- **Unit converter, angles**: type an angle as degrees-minutes-seconds, % slope, slope 1 in n, taper per foot, taper ratio 1:x or rate of taper, and see it in every angle unit; any angle is also shown as DMS, slope and taper with the half angle. Factors are exact (1 gon = 0.9°); tests against ISO/R 1119 (1:20 = 2°51′51.1″), the grade-of-slope examples and the NPT taper.
- `benchmate-core`: `units::dms_to_deg`, `angle_form_from_deg`, `angle_form_to_deg` with tests.
- **Bandsaw cut time**: single piece or bundle (pieces across and layers high) for round bar, rectangle and tube: metal area, feed height, effective width, cut time from the down-feed and from the cutting rate, and time per piece. Cut time = metal area / cutting rate (Starrett Bulletin 193; Machinery's Handbook example 5.5 in² at 4 in²/min = 1.375 min as a test).
- `benchmate-core`: `saw_section` and `saw_cut_time` with tests (the section area was worked out in the screen before).
- **Part-off and groove time** (`partofftime`, Turning): time to part off, face or plunge from a start to an end diameter with feed per rev, at constant surface speed with a G50 / G92 spindle limit (taken from the active machine when left blank) or at constant rpm. Shows the diameter where the limit is reached, the time above and below it, the rpm at both ends, and the time at constant start rpm for comparison.
- `benchmate-core`: `partoff_time` (closed form in two regions, checked against numerical integration and Isakov's "about half the constant-rpm time") and `partoff_time_rpm`, with tests. The multi-pass turning time tool already had the spindle limit, so it is unchanged.
- **Bolt circle**: a table of every hole (number, angle, X, Y) with **Copy table** (for a spreadsheet) and **Copy as X Y lines** (to paste into a program), plus the angle between holes and the centre-to-centre distance of neighbouring holes. The decimals buttons apply to the table.
- **Gauge-block stack**: wear blocks. Enter the size of your wear blocks and the stack is built with one at each end and the fewest blocks from your set between them. The set you own, your own block list and the wear block size are remembered.
- **Workspace pages**: show the tools as cards or as a compact list (remembered).
### Changed (improved tools)
- **Drill size chart**: the nearest-drill finder now takes a diameter in inch or mm (its own unit choice) and shows, for each of the number, letter, fractional and metric series, the nearest drill at or below and above, with the difference in inch and mm and an "exact" mark.
- The spot and centre drill tool is now its own screen (it was a plain form) so each table value can show its sources.

### Added (mechanics)
- New **Mechanics** group (parity batch B8). **Section properties**: area, centroid, Ix, Iy, product of inertia, principal second moments and axis angle, section modulus at each fibre, radii of gyration (and J for round bar and tube) for rectangle, hollow rectangle, round bar, tube, I-beam, channel, angle and tee, built up from rectangles (sharp corners), with a drawing.
- **Beam deflection and bending stress**: cantilever, simply supported and fixed both ends; point load anywhere and a uniform load (either or both); reactions, end moments, largest shear, moment, deflection and slope with their positions, span/deflection, M c / I; drawing of the deflected shape and the moment diagram. Shape fields appear only for the chosen section (new optional `when(v)` on registry fields).
- **Shaft torsion and power**: shear stress (outside and bore), angle of twist, stiffness, power from torque and speed or torque from power, G from E and Poisson's ratio, solid diameter for an allowable stress.
- **Column buckling**: Euler and Johnson with the switch-over at sqrt(2 pi^2 E / Sy), end conditions with theoretical K and the AISC recommended design values noted, allowable and applied load, column curve drawing.
- **Stress concentration (holes)**: round hole in a finite-width plate (net-section fit printed by fracturemechanics.org), elliptical hole (Inglis), Kf from your notch sensitivity. Stepped shafts, grooves and keyways are shown as not included: no free source prints a fit we could check.
- **Fatigue**: estimated endurance limit (labelled as an estimate), Marin surface and size factors from a free source, your own kc, kd, ke and Kf; factor of safety by modified Goodman, Soderberg, Gerber and first-cycle yield, with a fatigue diagram.
- **Centrifugal force and chuck grip**: F = m r omega^2 for any mass, jaw centrifugal force, grip left at speed for OD and ID gripping, speeds where one third and none of the grip remain, highest speed for the grip you need; with an explanation of grip loss.
- **Balancing (ISO 21940-11 grade G)**: permissible residual unbalance (g·mm, and oz·in in inch mode), centre-of-mass offset, two-plane split with the 30-70 % check, correction mass, force at speed, grade reached by a measured unbalance.
- **Critical speed of a shaft**: between bearings or overhung, up to three discs and the shaft's own mass; Rayleigh (upper) and Dunkerley (lower) estimates, exact plain-shaft value, static sag; assumptions on screen.
- `benchmate-core` module `mechanics` with 12 tests (AWC Design Aid 6 maxima, E I y'' = -M consistency of every beam case, Engineers Edge Marin check rows, Penn State/Norton Goodman example, IRD balancing example, exact single-disc and plain-shaft critical speeds); WebAssembly exports `mech_*`; formula library entries; `docs/mechanics.md`.
### Added (inspection round 2)

### Added (G-code backplot)
- **G-code backplot (simulator)** (G-code cycles group): paste or load a mill or lathe program and see the programmed path in 2D, XY / XZ / YZ for the mill and Z against X radius for the lathe (diameter halved), rapids dashed, feeds solid; step, scrub or play through the blocks with the current line highlighted in the listing; zoom and pan. Statistics: feed and rapid distance, extents of all moves and of feed moves, feed, rapid and dwell time and the estimated cycle time (feed per minute, or per revolution with S, G96 with the G50 clamp; acceleration, tool changes and G28 travel ignored). Messages list every error and warning with its line number.
- Reads a practical subset of Generic ISO 6983 / Fanuc-style code (Haas the same; other controls shown as not yet supported): G0-G3 with I/J/K or R and helical arcs, G4, G17/G18/G19, G20/G21 switches, G90/G91, G94/G95 and lathe G98/G99, G96/G97/G50, G28 to the intermediate point, F/S/T/M/N words, bracket and semicolon comments, lathe X diameter with U/W. An arc whose start and end radius differ by more than the tolerance (0.005 mm / 0.0002 in, editable) is an error. Mill G73, G74, G81-G86 and G89 are drawn hole by hole; lathe G70 runs its P..Q contour and G90/G92/G94 are drawn as single cycles; G71-G76 and other cycles are reported as "not simulated yet". Clearly a backplot, not a machine verification.
- `benchmate-core` module `gsim` (parser, modal state, arcs, cycles, time) with tests: every golden program of the cycle generators reads back with no errors and the same feed and dwell time as the generator's own moves, arc maths (I/J/K against R, major arcs, full circles, helix, G18/G19, radius mismatch), incremental mode, unit switch, lathe diameter and CSS timing; WebAssembly exports `gsim_*`; screen in `app/gsim.js`; see `docs/gsim.md`.

- **Unit converter**: 17 quantities (length, area, volume, mass, force, torque, pressure and stress, power, energy, speed, feed per minute, feed per rev, temperature, temperature difference, density, flow, angle), every unit at once, factors built from NIST SP 811 exact definitions.
- **Calculator**: typed expressions with brackets, powers, trig in degrees or radians, roots, logs, factorials, nCr, involute and DMS, parsed by our own parser in the Rust core (no eval), with error position; degrees-minutes-seconds converter, prime factors and linear interpolation.
- **Areas and volumes**: 10 plane figures (including regular polygons from across flats or across corners) and 11 solids, with drawings. **Compound angles**: true angle and slope direction from two drawing angles or two successive tilts, and the settings for a swivel and tilt, a compound sine plate (with block heights) or the two views.
